## What GEO Actually Is

GEO is not a rebrand of SEO. Search Engine Optimization optimizes for crawlers and ranking algorithms. Generative Engine Optimization optimizes for **citation** — the moment an LLM decides your brand is authoritative enough to mention by name.

The mechanics differ in three important ways:

1. **Entity clarity.** AI systems resolve brands as knowledge graph nodes. Ambiguous names, thin About pages, and inconsistent NAP data make you harder to cite.
2. **Answer architecture.** LLMs prefer content structured as direct, quotable claims — not keyword-stuffed paragraphs buried under hero images.
3. **Authority corroboration.** A single website is rarely enough. Reviews, press mentions, structured data, and third-party listings reinforce the same facts from multiple sources.

Traditional SEO chases position. GEO chases **inclusion** — being named in the generated response.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is Generative Engine Optimization in simple terms?**

GEO is the practice of making your brand easy for AI systems like ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews to find, understand, and cite by name when users ask questions in your category. It combines entity clarity, structured data, and authoritative content — not just traditional keyword optimization.

**Is GEO replacing SEO?**

No. SEO and GEO are complementary. SEO captures traffic from search result pages; GEO captures visibility inside AI-generated answers. The best growth strategies invest in both, especially as AI interfaces absorb more commercial research sessions in 2026.

**How long does GEO take to show results?**

Most clients see initial AI citations within 6–10 weeks of implementing entity foundations, structured data, and answer-engine content. Full compounding effects — consistent citations across multiple AI platforms — typically emerge over 4–6 months, similar to SEO timelines but with less competition today.

**Can I do GEO myself or do I need an agency?**

Foundational steps — consistent NAP data, basic schema markup, an `llms.txt` file — are DIY-friendly.
